SOP 01

IT Security Policy Guidelines

Purpose: Protect confidential research, employee information, systems, and network services against unauthorised access, alteration, loss, and disruption.

  • Use an individual account; never share passwords or approve an unexpected multi-factor authentication request.
  • Use long, unique passwords and multi-factor authentication wherever available.
  • Lock the workstation whenever leaving it unattended; sign out at the end of the workday.
  • Access only the systems and data required for your role. Managers must request access changes when duties change.
  • Store research and personal information only in approved, access-controlled locations.
  • Encrypt approved portable devices and protect them from theft; report a lost device immediately.
  • Do not install unapproved software, disable security controls, or connect unknown USB devices.
  • Apply approved updates and endpoint protection; do not bypass security warnings.
  • Verify unusual payment, password-reset, or data requests through a separate trusted channel.
  • Report suspected phishing, malware, unauthorised access, or data disclosure to the service desk immediately.
  • Keep access logs and security records only as long as required by the retention schedule and applicable law.
  • IT must review privileged accounts regularly and remove access promptly when employment ends.

Non-compliance: Access may be suspended while an incident is investigated. Disciplinary and legal action may apply in line with organisational policy.

SOP 02

Acceptable Use Policy

Purpose: Ensure the facility's technology is used safely, lawfully, and primarily for authorised organisational and research activities.

  • Use facility computers, Wi-Fi, email, printers, and research software for authorised work.
  • Keep research systems available for legitimate work; avoid unnecessary downloads or bandwidth-heavy activity.
  • Do not access, copy, alter, or disclose another person's files without permission.
  • Do not use IT resources for harassment, discrimination, illegal activity, copyright infringement, or offensive material.
  • Do not share confidential information through personal email, public file-sharing sites, or unapproved messaging tools.
  • Do not install applications or browser extensions without IT approval.
  • Do not attempt to bypass access controls, scan systems without authorisation, or test security tools without written approval.
  • Use only approved removable media and scan it before use.
  • Report accidental disclosure, suspicious messages, lost equipment, or unusual system behaviour promptly.
  • Personal use, where permitted, must be limited, lawful, and must not interfere with work or create security risks.
  • Users must follow data-classification, retention, copyright, and privacy requirements, including POPIA where applicable.
  • Return equipment and transfer work records when leaving the facility or changing roles.

SOP 03

IT Acquisition Procedures and Approvals

Purpose: Ensure purchases meet operational needs, are secure and compatible, remain within budget, and are recorded for support and audit.

  1. Submit a request: The staff member or department manager logs a request stating the business need, quantity, specifications, location, required date, and funding code.
  2. Technical assessment: IT checks compatibility, network capacity, licensing, cybersecurity, warranty, support, and whether existing equipment can be reused.
  3. Budget approval: The department manager confirms the need and budget. The finance/procurement function confirms funding and applicable procurement rules.
  4. Security and privacy review: Any solution that stores or processes research or personal information must be reviewed for access control, encryption, data location, backups, and contractual safeguards.
  5. Supplier quotations: Procurement obtains the required quotations and evaluates total cost of ownership, warranty, delivery, support, and compliance with purchasing rules.
  6. Authorisation: The authorised budget holder and procurement approver approve the purchase before an order is placed. Staff may not self-approve purchases.
  7. Receipt and inspection: IT verifies quantities, specifications, condition, licence details, and delivery documents before acceptance.
  8. Asset registration: Record asset tag, serial number, assigned user, location, purchase date, warranty, configuration, and disposal status in the asset register.
  9. Deployment: IT applies the standard secure configuration, updates, endpoint protection, access controls, and approved software before handing equipment to the user.
  10. Records and review: Store the request, approvals, quotations, purchase order, invoice, acceptance record, and licence evidence for audit.

Emergency purchases: Use the documented emergency approval route; record the reason and obtain retrospective review within the organisation's required period.

SOP 04

Service Desk / Help Desk Procedures

Purpose: Provide a consistent, traceable method for restoring service and resolving user requests.

  1. Submit a ticket through the approved service desk channel or contact the desk by phone for a critical outage.
  2. Include your name, department, contact details, device or asset tag, location, time noticed, symptoms, business impact, and any error message. Never include a password.
  3. The service desk records a unique ticket number, confirms receipt, categorises the issue, and assigns priority.
  4. P1 Critical: Facility-wide or critical research service outage, suspected active compromise, or major data risk. Notify the IT Manager immediately.
  5. P2 High: Multiple users or an important service affected, with no reasonable workaround. Escalate promptly to the relevant technical lead.
  6. P3 Normal: One user or a non-critical function affected. Queue and resolve according to service targets.
  7. P4 Request: Routine access, installation, or information request. Complete after the required approvals.
  8. Support staff diagnose the issue, document actions, protect evidence where security is involved, and escalate when access or expertise is insufficient.
  9. For suspected security incidents, follow the incident response process; do not delete evidence, wipe devices, or investigate beyond authorisation.
  10. Keep the requester informed of status, workaround, expected next update, and any required action.
  11. Test the fix with the requester where possible, document the resolution, obtain confirmation, and close the ticket.
  12. Review recurring incidents for root causes and update the knowledge base or change process as needed.

SOP 05

Disaster Recovery and Business Continuity

Purpose: Restore essential IT services safely after disruption while protecting research integrity, confidentiality, and availability.

Preparation and recovery targets

  • Maintain an inventory of critical services: research management server, identity/DHCP/DNS services, core network equipment, laboratory connectivity, file storage, and internet access.
  • Suggested initial targets, to be approved by management: critical research management service RTO of 4 hours and RPO of 1 hour; standard office services RTO of 24 hours and RPO of 24 hours.
  • Back up critical data daily at minimum, with more frequent backups where the approved RPO requires it. Maintain encrypted, access-controlled off-site or immutable copies.
  • Test restoration at least quarterly and after major system changes. Document results, gaps, and corrective actions.
  • Maintain current network diagrams, IP address plans, configuration backups, software/licence records, vendor contacts, and emergency contact details.

Response plans

  1. Server or storage failure: Notify the service desk; IT isolates faulty hardware, checks backup integrity, restores to approved replacement infrastructure, validates application and data consistency, and records the recovery.
  2. Ransomware or cyberattack: Isolate affected devices and network segments, notify the IT Manager and incident response lead, preserve logs and evidence, disable compromised accounts, assess scope, and restore only from verified clean backups after containment.
  3. Network or internet outage: Check power, core switches, routing, uplinks, and provider status. Use approved redundancy where available, communicate affected services, and verify connectivity and DHCP operation before declaring recovery.
  4. Power failure, fire, or physical disaster: Prioritise human safety; follow evacuation and facility emergency procedures. Do not enter unsafe areas. Activate alternate workspace or recovery site and restore critical services in agreed order.
  5. Accidental deletion or data corruption: Stop changes that may overwrite recoverable data, identify the affected dataset and time, restore to a separate location for validation, obtain data-owner approval, and record what was restored.
  6. Loss of a laboratory workstation: Disable the device's credentials or certificates, assess whether sensitive data was stored locally, replace the device from the standard build, restore approved files, and document the incident.
  7. Staff unavailability: Maintain named primary and backup owners for critical services, protected emergency access, and current runbooks so recovery does not depend on one person.

Recovery sequence

Declare incident → protect people and contain the incident → assess impact → notify management and affected users → activate recovery plan → restore network and identity services → restore critical research application and validated data → test security and functionality → obtain service-owner approval → communicate recovery → complete a post-incident review.

IT GOVERNANCE

IT Department Structure and Reporting Lines

Facility Director / Senior ManagementApproves budget, risk acceptance, and major purchases
IT ManagerReports to Facility Director
Network AdministratorReports to IT Manager; LAN, switches, routing, DHCP, Wi-Fi
Systems AdministratorReports to IT Manager; servers, accounts, backups, recovery
Service Desk TechnicianReports to IT Manager; tickets, endpoints, user support
Application / Data Support OfficerReports to IT Manager; research software, access coordination, data-owner liaison

For a small facility, some roles may be combined, but responsibilities and approval separation must remain clear. External vendors report through the IT Manager or the designated contract owner.

Role responsibilities

  • Facility Director: Approves policy, funding, risk decisions, and major continuity priorities.
  • IT Manager: Owns IT operations, staff allocation, policy enforcement, risk escalation, procurement recommendations, and disaster recovery coordination.
  • Network Administrator: Maintains switching, routing, VLANs, DHCP, addressing, network security, monitoring, and configuration backups.
  • Systems Administrator: Maintains servers, identity services, patching, endpoint management, backup jobs, restore tests, and privileged access.
  • Service Desk Technician: Logs and prioritises incidents, supports users, maintains ticket records, and escalates technical or security incidents.
  • Application / Data Support Officer: Coordinates research software support, approved access, application testing, and communication with research data owners.
  • Department managers / data owners: Approve business access needs and confirm that staff access remains appropriate.
